# Sequence Architecture

One email is a coin flip. A sequence is a system. This is the 4-step structure
every campaign I run uses, with the timing, the threading decisions, and the job
each step exists to do.

The structure exists to squeeze two genuine tests out of one campaign: steps 1-2
test one offer angle, steps 3-4 test a second one, on the same list, without the
prospect ever feeling chased.

## When to use this

- Turning approved copy variants into an actual campaign structure
- Deciding follow-up timing for any campaign
- Reviewing a sequence someone else built ("why is step 2 one day later?")
- Deciding whether a follow-up threads on the previous email or starts fresh

You need finished copy first. If you don't have variants yet, run
`/cold-email-copywriting` and come back.

## The structure

```
Step 1   day 0      first touch          new thread, lowercase subject
Step 2   +3 days    nudge                SAME thread, no subject
Step 3   +30 days   second first touch   NEW thread, NEW subject, NEW offer angle
Step 4   +3 days    nudge on step 3      SAME thread as step 3, no subject
```

Two hard timing rules:

1. **Minimum 3 days between any two steps. Always.** No exceptions, no "day 2
   bump". Anything tighter reads as pressure to the prospect and as a volume
   pattern to spam filters. Three days also means a Thursday send doesn't chase
   itself into the weekend.
2. **The gap before step 3 is long: around 30 days.** This is not laziness, it's
   the mechanism. After a month, the first thread is gone from inbox memory, so
   a new thread with a new subject and a new angle genuinely reads as a first
   touch, not attempt number three. Short-gap "new threads" fool nobody.

All subjects lowercase, 2-4 words, per the copywriting house style. Steps 2 and
4 have NO subject at all: they inherit the thread's subject by replying in it.

## What each step's job is

### Step 1: the test

Your best shot at the chosen offer angle, at full quality. Strongest variant
set, best proof point, cleanest CTA. Everything `/cold-email-copywriting`
produced for this angle goes here. If the campaign is going to work, this step
is where it shows.

### Step 2: the nudge (same thread)

Short. Shorter than step 1. Its only job is to resurface the thread for people
who saw the first email at a bad moment. Rules:

- Re-ask the SAME offer with different words. No new offer, no new claims.
- Never open with "just following up", "bumping this", or any reference to the
  previous email as an apology. The thread position already says "follow-up";
  the words don't need to.
- If step 1 ran naked (no proof line), step 2 is the natural place for the
  credibility line. That's a real reason to exist beyond "nudge".
- 2-3 sentences is plenty. An interest check and out.

### Step 3: the second first touch (new thread, new angle)

This is the step most people get wrong. Step 3 is NOT "follow-up number three".
It is a brand new first email that happens to go to the same list:

- **New thread. New lowercase subject.**
- **New offer angle**, pulled from your `/offer-testing-roadmap`. If steps 1-2
  offered the quoting playbook, step 3 offers the case study breakdown, or the
  sample asset, or a different KPI entirely.
- **Zero references to the earlier emails.** Not "I reached out a while back".
  Nothing. It must stand completely alone; a prospect who never saw thread one
  should find nothing odd about it.
- Written to full step-1 quality. Same house style, same bans, same 50-90 words.

Why this works: the prospect who ignored angle A isn't necessarily cold, they're
often just not an angle-A buyer. Step 3 is your second test on the same list,
at zero extra list cost.

### Step 4: the nudge on step 3 (same thread as step 3)

Same rules as step 2, applied to the step-3 thread. Short re-ask of the step-3
angle, no new claims, credibility line if step 3 didn't carry one. After step 4,
the sequence ends. No step 5 "breakup email" theatrics; if two angles and two
nudges didn't land, the answer is a different offer or a different list, not a
guilt trip.

## Thread vs fresh: the decision rule

- **Thread (no subject)** when the step's job is to resurface an existing
  conversation about the SAME offer. Steps 2 and 4.
- **Fresh (new subject)** when the step carries a NEW offer angle. Step 3, and
  only after a long gap. A new angle in an old thread wastes the angle: the
  thread's context anchors them to the thing they already ignored.

Never do the reverse. A same-offer nudge in a new thread looks like spray; a
new offer buried in an old thread never gets seen.

## Worked example (fictional company)

Freightline Labs (fictional ops consultancy for freight brokerages). Angle A is
the same-day quoting playbook; angle B, from the testing roadmap, is a dispatch
case study.

**Step 1, day 0.** Subject: `quote turnaround`

```
{{FIRST_NAME}}, if I put together a same-day quoting playbook for {{COMPANY}},
would that be useful?

We've helped 60+ freight brokerages cut quote turnaround from days to hours
without changing their TMS.

Reply "yes" and I'll send it across.

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Freightline Labs
```

**Step 2, +3 days.** Same thread, no subject.

```
{{FIRST_NAME}}, the playbook covers the three workflow changes that matter, in
order, with the numbers at each step.

Want me to send it over?

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Freightline Labs
```

**Step 3, +30 days.** NEW thread. Subject: `dispatch playbooks`

```
{{FIRST_NAME}}, just built {{COMPANY}} a sample dispatch playbook: one page per

One brokerage running this format cut check-in calls by half in six weeks.

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Freightline Labs

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Freightline Labs
```

## Common mistakes

1. **Gaps under 3 days.** A day-1 bump reads as desperation and patterns as
   spam. Three days minimum, between every pair of steps, always.
2. **Step 3 as "one more follow-up" in the same thread.** You just spent your
   second test on a thread they already ignored.
3. **Step 3 referencing earlier emails.** "Circling back on my last note" turns
   a fresh first impression into attempt three.
4. **Same angle across all four steps.** Four sends, one data point. The
   structure exists to get you two.
5. **Step 2 longer than step 1.** The nudge is not the place for the essay.
6. **Sentence-case or 6-word subjects.** Lowercase, 2-4 words, looks like a
   colleague sent it.
7. **Writing a subject line for steps 2 and 4.** Threaded steps inherit the
   subject; giving them one breaks the thread.
8. **Adding steps 5-8 to a cold sequence.** Past step 4 you're burning list and
   domain reputation. Warm follow-up (after a REPLY) is a different system with
   different rules; that lives in `/reply-followups`.
