# Reply Follow-ups

An interested reply that goes quiet is not a dead lead. It's someone who raised their hand and then got busy. Most senders either give up after one "just checking in" or bump the thread into oblivion with content-free nudges. Both waste the most expensive thing a cold campaign produces: a human who already said they're interested.

My system is 8 follow-ups, each one a real email with something new in it. Not eight bumps. Eight fresh reasons to book.

## When to use this

- A prospect replied interested, you answered, and they've gone silent for 3+ days
- You're building the follow-up set in advance so it's ready the moment a thread stalls (do this, it's how it should be done)
- Your current follow-ups are variations of "just floating this to the top of your inbox" and you know it
- You're deciding whether to keep chasing or stop

This is for people who REPLIED and stalled. Follow-up steps inside the cold sequence itself are a different thing, covered in sequence-architecture.

## The three laws

**1. Every touch brings a NEW angle.** Never "just bumping this", never "did you see my last email", never "circling back". Each follow-up stands alone as a piece of insight they could get value from without ever booking. If a follow-up contains no new idea, it's a nag, and nags train people to ignore the thread.

**2. Every touch names specific proof with a number.** First person, named case study, exact result. "I rebuilt one client's checkout flow and mobile conversion went from 1.1% to 2.4% in five weeks" beats "we've helped companies improve conversion" every single time. No exceptions to this rule: all eight touches carry a case study with a number. And never reuse the same case study in consecutive touches, spread your proof across the set.

**3. Every touch ends on a varied CTA around the calendar link.** The booking link appears in every follow-up as a clickable hyperlink on natural anchor text, but the sentence around it changes every time. Never the same CTA structure twice in a row.

## The mechanics

- **Spacing: minimum 3 days between touches.** My cadence: roughly days 3, 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26, 30 after your reply. The gaps widen as the sequence ages. Never tighter than 3 days, pressure reads as desperation.
- **Send as thread replies**, on the same conversation. The context of their original interest is the asset; don't throw it away with a new thread.
- **These sends are HTML, not plain text.** This matters and people get it wrong. Cold sends should be plain text, but follow-up replies must be HTML so the calendar link stays a clickable anchor. If your sending tool flattens these to plain text, every prospect gets a dead link and the whole system is pointless. Check this setting before the first follow-up goes out.
- **Length: 150 words max per follow-up.** The final touch is shorter, 50 to 70 words.
- **Any reply exits the sequence immediately.** The moment they respond, stop the automation and go back to live conversation (reply-drafting rules apply).

## The per-touch pattern

Every one of the eight follows the same three-beat shape:

1. A new idea, insight, or angle (1 to 2 sentences, an actual thesis, not "X is important")
2. "I did [specific thing] for [named case study] and the result was [specific number]." (1 sentence)
3. A CTA that varies each time, ending on the hyperlinked calendar link (1 sentence)

## The eight angles

These are the themes I run, in order. Adapt each to your offer and your proof.

1. **What actually moves the needle.** The thing most buyers in their space get wrong about your category, and what the real unlock is.
2. **Why you can promise what you promise.** The mechanism behind your guarantee or speed claim, why it's structural, not a sales line.
3. **The risky status quo.** The comfortable-but-fragile way they do it today versus the predictable alternative.
4. **Capacity.** They probably know what to do; the bottleneck is time and hands to do it at volume.
5. **Old model vs new model.** The expensive old way versus the leaner way you work now.
6. **The parallel case study.** Pick your case study most like their situation, lead with the parallel, walk the result.
7. **The counterintuitive insight.** Something that runs against conventional wisdom in their space.
8. **The soft close.** 50 to 70 words. No new pitch. Confidence, not desperation: the offer stands, circle back whenever, link.

>

>

>
> Joel

**Touch 6 (day 22, angle: parallel case study):**

> Hey Tom,
>
> Your setup reminds me of Rowan Home Goods, similar catalogue size, same platform, same third-party app pile-up slowing everything down.
>
> We stripped and rebuilt their stack and conversion went from 1.4% to 2.2% inside six weeks, no redesign, same brand.
>
> Happy to show you exactly what we changed and what would carry over: [pick a time here].
>
> Joel

**Touch 8 (day 30, the soft close):**

> Hey Tom,
>
> Last one from me, timing clearly isn't right and that's fine.
>
> The offer stands whenever it is: the audit that found Aldergate 31% more mobile revenue is yours if you want it.
>

>
> Joel

Notice: three different CTAs, three different angles, a named number in every touch, none of them says "bumping".

## When to stop

- **After touch 8, stop.** The soft close is the close. More than eight is harassment, and the soft close only works if it's actually the last one.
- **Any "no", stop immediately.** "Not interested" or "please stop" ends the sequence that second, no clever final touch.
- **Unsubscribe or opt-out, stop and remove them everywhere**, not just this thread.
- **A referral ends this sequence.** "Talk to our COO instead" means stop emailing this person and start a fresh, separate outreach to the named one.
- **A booking obviously ends it.** Make sure your process actually removes booked prospects from the queue; a follow-up landing after they've booked makes you look like a machine, because at that point you were.

## Common mistakes

- **"Just bumping this."** The cardinal sin. Zero new information teaches them the thread is safe to ignore.
- **Vague proof.** "We've helped similar companies" carries no weight. Named case study, exact number, every touch.
- **Same CTA every touch.** "Worth a chat?" eight times reads like a broken robot.
- **Plain-text follow-ups.** Dead calendar links, silent booking killer. These sends are HTML.
- **Gaps under 3 days.** Eagerness reads as desperation and burns the goodwill their reply created.
- **Reusing one case study back to back.** Spread the proof, consecutive repeats scream "that's all they've got".
- **Not stopping.** Continuing past a no, past an unsubscribe, or past touch 8 turns a warm lead into someone who tells peers about you for the wrong reason.

## What to do next

If a follow-up lands a reply, drop out of the sequence and run `/reply-drafting` to answer it properly. If lots of interested prospects are going quiet after your FIRST reply, the problem may be the reply itself, review it against `/reply-drafting` before blaming the follow-ups.
