# Reply Drafting

The reply is where cold email is won or lost. The campaign's only job was to get a human to respond. Now a real person is looking at their inbox, mildly curious, and the next message decides whether that curiosity becomes a meeting or evaporates. Most people fumble it in one of two ways: they gatekeep (withhold the thing they promised to force a call) or they over-template (send something that obviously wasn't written for this person). Both kill the booking.

These are my rules. They're short because good replies are short.

## When to use this

- Any reply classified as interested or objection needs an answer (run reply-classification first if you haven't)
- You're about to answer a pricing question, a technical question, or a "yes, send it over"
- A prospect pushed back and you need to handle it without sounding like a sales script
- You want to sanity-check a reply draft before it goes out

## The rules

**1. Reply fast.** Interest decays by the hour. The prospect replied in a moment of curiosity; answer while that moment is still alive. Aim for minutes, not days. A mediocre reply in 10 minutes beats a perfect reply tomorrow.

**2. Deliver the promise.** If your cold email offered something (findings, an audit, a teardown, a list of ideas) and they said yes, the reply DELIVERS it. Immediately, in the reply itself. Never "great, let's hop on a call and I'll walk you through it". That's a bait-and-switch and they feel it instantly. Give the goods, then make the call the natural next step for going deeper. Generosity books more meetings than gatekeeping ever has.

**3. Soften the time ask.** The cold email never mentioned a call, time, or a meeting (that's the copy doctrine, see cold-email-copywriting). The reply is where the call enters, and it enters gently: "only takes 10 minutes", "won't take long", "happy to walk you through it whenever suits". You're easing them toward time, not demanding it.

**4. Propose two exact time slots.** "When works for you?" creates work for them, and work kills momentum. Offer two specific, real slots from your calendar, one near, one a few days out, then a catch-all link:

> Does tomorrow at 9am or Thursday at 2pm work for you (London time)? If neither works, grab another time here.

**5. The calendar link is visible and clickable.** In replies, the booking link goes in as a proper hyperlink on natural anchor text: "grab a time here", "pick a slot here", "here's the link". Never a bare pasted URL, never "book a call" as the anchor. This is the opposite of cold email, where links never appear. Post-reply you've earned the link; use it on every reply, even soft ones (a quiet signature-line link on a "not now" costs nothing).

**6. Match their energy and length.** Three-word reply gets a short, punchy answer. Detailed technical paragraph gets a proper considered response. Never send four paragraphs to someone who wrote "sure, send it". 100 words is the ceiling for most replies; under 50 is usually better.

**7. Never over-templatize.** Shapes are fine, scripts are not. Every reply should read like it was written for this person after actually reading what they said, because it was. Answer their actual words, reference their actual company, respond to their actual question. If your reply would work pasted under any other prospect's message, it's not done.

## Step-by-step process

1. **Read their reply properly.** What did they actually say, what did they actually ask? Not the nearest scenario, their words.
2. **Check what your cold email promised.** The reply has to make good on it. Pull the asset, findings, or answer you owe them.
3. **Draft the shape:** acknowledge what they said, answer or deliver, then frame the next step softly with two exact slots and a clickable link.
4. **Cut it down.** Match their length and energy. Remove every sentence that isn't acknowledging, delivering, or moving to the next step.
5. **Check the tone.** Casual, confident, a smart human. No corporate filler, no "I hope this email finds you well", no exclamation-mark enthusiasm, no em dashes.
6. **Send it now**, not after lunch.

## Worked examples

All companies and people below are fictional. Slots and links are placeholders; use real ones from your calendar.

### Hard yes

They wrote: *"Sure, send it over."* Your email promised three specific ideas for improving Harbourline's onboarding flow.

> Nice one, Dana.
>
> Here are the three:
>
> 1. Your signup asks for a team size before showing any product value. Flip the order and completion should jump.
> 2. The empty dashboard on first login is a dead end. A pre-filled sample project would give people something to touch in the first minute.
> 3. Your activation email goes out on day 3. The data on this is brutal, day 1 or it barely converts.
>
> Happy to walk through how I'd implement each one, only takes 15 minutes. Does tomorrow at 10am or Friday at 3pm work (London time)? If neither does, [grab another time here].
>
> Joel

Delivered in full, no gatekeeping, call framed as the way to go deeper.

### Info request

They wrote: *"How does the pricing work?"*

> Good question, Marcus.
>
> Flat monthly fee, no long contract, and the first month is scoped so you can see output before committing further. For a team Bluepine's size it usually lands in the low four figures.
>
> Easiest way to give you an exact number is a quick look at what you're running now, 10 minutes tops. Does tomorrow at 9am or Monday at 1pm suit (New York time)? If not, [pick a slot here].
>
> Joel

Answered the actual question with a real posture, exact number tied to a short call for a legitimate reason.

### Technical question

They wrote: *"Does this integrate with our HubSpot setup? We've got a lot of custom pipelines."*

> It does, Sam. Everything writes to HubSpot natively, and custom pipelines are fine, you map each stage once during setup and it stays in sync from there.
>
> Custom properties carry over too, which is usually the bit people worry about.
>
> If it'd help, I can show you the mapping on a shared screen, takes about 10 minutes. Tomorrow at 11am or Thursday at 4pm any good (London time)? Otherwise [grab a time here].
>
> Joel

Direct technical answer first. The call is offered as a convenience, not a toll booth.

### Soft interest

They wrote: *"Potentially interesting. What would this actually look like for us?"*

> Fair question, Elena.
>
> For Fernwood it'd be: we map your current outbound in week one, rebuild the list and copy in week two, and you'd see the first replies land inside the first fortnight. You'd approve everything before it sends.
>
> Easiest to show you a live example of what we built for a similar team, won't take long. Does tomorrow at 2pm or Friday at 10am work (Berlin time)? If neither, [here's the link].
>
> Joel

Concrete and specific to them. Soft interest hardens on specifics and dies on vagueness.

### Objection

They wrote: *"We already work with an agency for this."*

> Fair enough, Priya, most teams I talk to do.
>
> Worth asking one thing: are you getting interested replies, or just sends? The gap I usually find isn't effort, it's the offer the emails make.
>
> If the numbers are good, genuinely, stick with them. If you're not sure, I'll take a look at what's running and tell you straight, 15 minutes. Tomorrow at 9:30am or Wednesday at 3pm (London time)? Or [pick a time here] if those don't fit.
>
> Joel

No trash-talking the incumbent, one sharp reframing question, a low-risk offer, and an honest out.

## Common mistakes

- **Gatekeeping the promise.** "Let's jump on a call and I'll share the findings" after you promised findings. Instant trust burn, the single biggest reply killer.
- **Slow replies.** A day-old reply is answering a colder person than the one who wrote to you.
- **"When works for you?"** Open-ended scheduling is friction. Two exact slots plus a link, every time.
- **Bare URLs or "book a call" anchors.** Hyperlink natural text.
- **Essay replies to one-line messages.** Match their energy or lose them.
- **Scenario-script pasting.** If the reply doesn't reference what they actually wrote, rewrite it.
- **Hard-selling an objection.** Objections want a straight answer and a low-pressure next step, not a rebuttal speech.
- **Putting a calendar link in the cold email itself.** Links live in replies, never in cold sends.
