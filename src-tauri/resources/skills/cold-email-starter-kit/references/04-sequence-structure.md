# 04: Sequence Structure

One email is a coin flip; a sequence is a system. This is the 4-step structure every campaign in this system uses. Its real trick: it squeezes two genuine offer tests out of one campaign. Steps 1-2 test angle A, steps 3-4 test angle B, on the same list, without the prospect ever feeling chased. Full doctrine in `/sequence-architecture`.

## The structure

```
Step 1   day 0      first touch          new thread, lowercase 2-4 word subject
Step 2   +3 days    nudge                SAME thread, no subject
Step 3   +30 days   second first touch   NEW thread, NEW subject, NEW offer angle
Step 4   +3 days    nudge on step 3      SAME thread as step 3, no subject
```

Two hard timing rules:

1. **Minimum 3 days between any two steps. Always.** No "day 2 bump". Tighter reads as pressure to the prospect and as a volume pattern to spam filters.
2. **The gap before step 3 is ~30 days, and that's the mechanism, not laziness.** After a month the first thread is gone from inbox memory, so a new thread with a new angle genuinely reads as a first touch. Short-gap "new threads" fool nobody.

## Each step's job

- **Step 1: the test.** Your best shot at angle A, full quality, strongest proof.
- **Step 2: the nudge.** Shorter than step 1. Re-asks the SAME offer in different words. Never "just following up" or any apology for existing; the thread position already says follow-up. If step 1 ran without a proof line, step 2 is the natural home for the credibility line.
- **Step 3: the second first touch.** NOT follow-up number three. A brand new first email carrying a NEW angle from your testing roadmap, with zero references to the earlier emails. A prospect who never saw thread one should find nothing odd about it. The prospect who ignored angle A often just isn't an angle-A buyer; this is your second test at zero extra list cost.
- **Step 4: the nudge on step 3.** Same rules as step 2. Then the sequence ENDS. No step 5, no "breakup email" theatrics; if two angles and two nudges didn't land, the fix is a different offer or list, not a guilt trip.

**Thread vs fresh, the rule:** thread (no subject) when re-asking the same offer; fresh (new subject) only for a new angle after a long gap. Never the reverse.

## A complete sequence you can adapt

Fictional: **Ledgerline**, payroll for mid-size construction firms (same company as `03-copywriting.md`). Angle A is the payroll leak check; angle B, from the testing roadmap, is a built cost-code cheat sheet.

**Step 1, day 0.** Subject: `payroll codes`

```
{{FIRST_NAME}}, if I ran a payroll leak check for {{COMPANY}} and sent over
what we find, no charge, would that be useful?

We've helped 30+ mid-size contractors claw back five figures a year in
miscoded overtime without switching payroll systems.

Reply "yes" and I'll get it moving.

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Ledgerline
```

**Step 2, +3 days.** Same thread, no subject.

```
{{FIRST_NAME}}, the leak check covers the four places contractor payroll
overpays: overtime codes, per diem handling, union tiers, and rework hours.

Takes nothing from your side to run.

Want me to get it started?

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Ledgerline
```

**Step 3, +30 days.** NEW thread. Subject: `cost codes`

```
{{FIRST_NAME}}, just built {{COMPANY}} a labor cost-code cheat sheet: one
page, the 20 codes mid-size contractors misuse most, with the correct
mapping next to each.

One estimator told us it ended a year-long argument with their PM team in
a week.

Want me to send it over?

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Ledgerline
```

New angle, new frame, no memory of the first thread.

**Step 4, +3 days.** Same thread as step 3, no subject.

```
{{FIRST_NAME}}, the cheat sheet's sitting here with {{COMPANY}}'s name on it.

Would it be useful if I sent it across?

{{SENDER_FIRST_NAME}}
Ledgerline
```

Every email obeys the `03-copywriting.md` rules: 50-90 words or fewer, name inline, interest-check CTAs, exact claims, no links, no time asks, lowercase subjects only where a subject exists at all.

## Common mistakes

1. **Gaps under 3 days.** Desperation to the human, spam pattern to the filter.
2. **Step 3 in the same thread.** You just spent your second test on a thread they already ignored.
3. **Step 3 referencing earlier emails.** "Circling back" turns a fresh first impression into attempt three.
4. **Same angle across all four steps.** Four sends, one data point. The structure exists to get you two.
5. **Writing subjects for steps 2 and 4.** Threaded steps inherit the subject; giving them one breaks the thread.
6. **Adding steps 5-8.** Past step 4 you're burning list and domain reputation. Follow-ups after a REPLY are a different system with different rules: `/reply-followups`.
